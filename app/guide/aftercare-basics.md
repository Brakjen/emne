# Aftercare Basics

Post-collection care is where most yamadori are lost. Follow these fundamentals.

## First 48 hours

1. Keep rootball moist—never let it dry out.
2. Mist foliage if sunny or windy.
3. Move to shade as soon as possible.
4. Pot into a recovery container with fast-draining substrate.

## Substrate for recovery

- Pure pumice, lava rock, or coarse akadama.
- Avoid organic mixes that hold too much water and invite rot.
- Tie the tree securely; root movement kills new roots.

## Environment

- **Shade**: No direct sun for 3–4 weeks minimum.
- **Wind protection**: Reduces transpiration stress.
- **Temperature**: Keep frost-free but cool if collected early season.

## Watering

- Water when the surface feels dry, not on a schedule.
- Overwatering causes root rot—drainage is critical.
- Mist foliage in dry weather but don't soak soil unnecessarily.

## Signs of recovery

- New buds extending normally.
- Fresh white root tips visible at drain holes (may take weeks).
- Foliage stays turgid without wilting.

## Signs of trouble

- Needles browning from tips inward.
- Bark wrinkling or feeling loose.
- No bud movement after expected time.

## When to resume normal care

- After one full growing season with strong new growth.
- Then gradually introduce more sun, light feeding, and eventual repotting.
