# Norwegian Soil Products Guide

Finding quality bonsai substrate in Norway can be challenging. This guide covers locally available products and practical substitutes.

## The problem with bark in Norway

Finely graded bark (2–6 mm) is hard to find. Garden centres stock coarse mulch, and sifting is tedious. Good news: bark is not essential. Several alternatives work as well or better.

## Lettklinker (LECA)

Expanded clay aggregate, sold at Plantasjen, Byggmakker, and hardware stores.

**Pros:**
- Cheap and widely available
- Excellent drainage
- Reusable after cleaning
- Neutral pH

**Cons:**
- Holds less water than pumice
- Light—pots can tip in wind
- Round shape means less root grip

**Best use:** Drainage layer, or mixed 20–30% into training soil for fast-draining species (pines, junipers).

**Particle size:** Buy the smallest grade (2–4 mm or "mini"). Sift out dust.

## Pimpstein (Pumice)

Available from aquarium shops, some garden centres, or online (e.g., bonsainorge.no).

**Pros:**
- Ideal water/air balance
- Roots grip the porous surface
- Does not break down

**Cons:**
- More expensive than LECA
- Heavier (can be a pro for stability)

**Best use:** Primary component in any mix. 50–70% pumice works for most species.

## Ready-to-use bark substitutes

These come in correct particle sizes without processing:

| Product | Where to buy | Notes |
|---------|--------------|-------|
| **Seramis** | Plantasjen, Europris | Baked clay granules, 2–4 mm. Holds moisture well. Good organic substitute. |
| **Orchid bark** | Garden centres | Often pre-sifted 6–12 mm. Use for larger pots or mix with fines. |
| **Reptile bark** | Dyrebutikker | Sometimes better graded than garden bark. Check particle size on bag. |
| **Coconut husk chips** | Online, reptile shops | 5–10 mm chips. Breaks down slower than bark. Rinse to remove salt. |
| **Sphagnum moss** | Plantasjen, florists | Chopped or long-fiber. Great for moisture retention, yamadori recovery. |

## Cat litter trick

Non-clumping, baked clay cat litter (e.g., "Catsan Hygiene Plus", some budget brands) is cheap fired clay—essentially the same as akadama.

**How to use:**
1. Check ingredients: must be 100% baked clay, no additives or clumping agents.
2. Sift to remove dust and get 2–5 mm particles.
3. Rinse before use.

**Caution:** Clumping litter will turn to mush. Only use non-clumping types.

## Simple training soil recipes

### General deciduous (bjørk, rogn, lønn)
- 50% pumice
- 30% Seramis or LECA
- 20% sphagnum or coconut chips

### Conifers (furu, gran, einer)
- 60% pumice
- 30% LECA
- 10% sphagnum (optional, for moisture)

### Yamadori recovery
- 70% pumice (fast drainage critical)
- 20% LECA
- 10% chopped sphagnum
- No bark or organics until established

### Budget mix
- 50% sifted LECA (2–4 mm)
- 30% sifted cat litter (baked clay)
- 20% Seramis

## Tips

- **Always sift** components to remove dust and get uniform particle size.
- **Test drainage**: water should flow through in seconds, not pool on top.
- **Label your mixes** so you remember what works.
- **Reuse inorganics**: LECA and pumice last forever—just wash between uses.
