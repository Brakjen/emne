# Collection Timing Guide

Timing your yamadori collection correctly dramatically increases survival rates. This guide covers the key principles.

## General rule

Collect during **late dormancy**, just before bud swell. The tree has stored energy but hasn't committed it to new growth yet.

## Regional timing (Norway / Scandinavia)

| Species group | Best window | Notes |
|---------------|-------------|-------|
| Pines (Scots, mountain) | Late March – early May | Before candles extend |
| Spruce | April – early May | Before buds open |
| Juniper | April – May or late summer | Very hardy; autumn possible |
| Deciduous (birch, rowan) | March – April | Before leaf-out |
| Larch | March – April | Before needles emerge |

## Signs to watch

- Ground is thawed enough to dig but nights still cold.
- Buds are swelling but not yet open.
- Sap is not yet running hard (avoid heavy bleeding on conifers).

## What to avoid

- Mid-summer: full growth, high transpiration, stress risk.
- Late autumn: insufficient time to establish roots before freeze.
- During or right after a drought/heat wave.

## Aftercare timing

- Keep collected trees in shade for 2–4 weeks.
- Protect from late frosts; cold frame or unheated garage works.
- Delay full sun and fertilizer until you see healthy new growth.
